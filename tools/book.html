Part IEleven years in the Dominican Republic

I was born in the United States and spent eleven years growing up in the Dominican Republic.1 That's where I learned to be resourceful. When you don't have everything handed to you, you get creative with what you have.

Growing up between two languages and two cultures taught me to adapt quickly, listen closely, and feel at home somewhere new. I still lean on that every day.

1The Dominican Republic shares the island of Hispaniola with Haiti. It is shaded red on the frontispiece.

Part IINew York, which doesn't wait for anyone

Moving to New York City was its own education.2 The pace and the ambition rewire you. New York taught me that if you want something, you go and get it. You don't wait for permission or a perfect plan.

2About 1,550 miles north of where I grew up, measured in a straight line.

Part IIIThe non-traditional path

My route to a four-year degree started at community college. Some people see that as a detour. For me it was a launchpad. I put my head down, excelled, and earned my transfer to Binghamton University.3

3Where you start doesn't decide where you finish.

Part IVBinghamton, where I fell for startups

I studied Financial Economics, but entrepreneurship is what grabbed me. A small group of people with an idea and enough grit could build something from nothing and put it in people's hands. I went to every talk I could find and started treating problems as things to build.

Binghamton University, State University of New York · B.S. Financial Economics · December 2015

Part VThe magic book

My first real startup was an augmented reality magic book: a printed book that came alive when you pointed a phone at it. Characters stepped off the page, and readers could explore a story instead of only reading it.

Partway through the build, my technical cofounder quit. Nobody was left to build it, so I decided to become someone who could.

I taught myself to code, one tutorial, one bug and one late night at a time.

Part VIFrom learning to code to shipping

Teaching myself turned into a habit of building. I've designed and built mobile apps and websites from the ground up, frontend and backend, and published iOS apps to the App Store.4

Tools of the trade: Python, JavaScript, React, iOS, SQL, REST APIs, Snowflake, Power BI and large language models.

4My own projects have run on Heroku and on AWS EC2.

Part VIIA builder inside finance and data

For ten years I've worked where product, data and technology meet, turning messy problems into products people use.

At Jefferies, as Product Strategist in Investment Banking, now Vice President, I led data scientists, engineers, delivery and business leaders to launch the firm's first LLM-powered application.5 I set strategy for a portfolio of fintech and banking software and use SQL-driven analysis to decide what to build next.

5Before that, as a Data Analyst at Jefferies, I ran SQL and Python analysis for M&A sell-side deals and automated the team's performance reporting.

At Avero, as Product Owner and Data Analyst, I owned the sales, onboarding and billing platform end to end. I published nationwide reporting on the restaurant industry's recovery after COVID and moved the company's data into Snowflake and Power BI.

Part VIIIAtriums AI

Outside my day job, I'm building again. Atriums AI6 is a live room where a team talks through an idea and AI coding agents build it while they talk. The group compares the versions side by side, votes, and pushes the winner to GitHub with a spec.

It's the same pull I felt at Binghamton and the same stubbornness from the magic book, now with ten years of product experience behind it.

From conversation to working prototype, in one meeting.

EpilogueWhat carries through

Learning

From the DR to self-taught code to LLMs, I'm happiest as a beginner at something new.

Pushing my limits

When my cofounder left, I learned to build it myself. I keep choosing problems a little bigger than I am.

Caring about the work

My name is on what I ship. I sweat the details because the people using it deserve that.
