A short history, in eight parts

Born in the United States, raised in the Dominican Republic, built in New York.

I'm Julius. I took a non-traditional path into technology and have been teaching myself to build ever since. Here is the route, with the numbers.

Part I Eleven years in the Dominican Republic

I was born in the United States and spent eleven years growing up in the Dominican Republic.1The Dominican Republic shares the island of Hispaniola with Haiti. It is shaded red on the chart above. That's where I learned to be resourceful. When you don't have everything handed to you, you get creative with what you have.

Growing up between two languages and two cultures taught me to adapt quickly, listen closely, and feel at home somewhere new. I still lean on that every day.

Part II New York, which doesn't wait for anyone

Moving to New York City was its own education. The pace and the ambition rewire you. New York taught me that if you want something, you go and get it. You don't wait for permission or a perfect plan.

Part III The non-traditional path

My route to a four-year degree started at community college. Some people see that as a detour. For me it was a launchpad. I put my head down, excelled, and earned my transfer to Binghamton University.2Where you start doesn't decide where you finish.

Part IV Binghamton, where I fell for startups

I studied Financial Economics, but entrepreneurship is what grabbed me. A small group of people with an idea and enough grit could build something from nothing and put it in people's hands. I went to every talk I could find and started treating problems as things to build.

Binghamton University, State University of New York · B.S. Financial Economics · December 2015

Part V The magic book

My first real startup was an augmented reality magic book: a printed book that came alive when you pointed a phone at it. Characters stepped off the page, and readers could explore a story instead of only reading it.

Partway through the build, my technical cofounder quit. Nobody was left to build it, so I decided to become someone who could. I taught myself to code, one tutorial, one bug and one late night at a time.

When you're blocked, learn your way through it.

The lesson I still work by

Part VI From learning to code to shipping

Teaching myself turned into a habit of building. I've designed and built mobile apps and websites from the ground up, frontend and backend, and published iOS apps to the App Store.3My own projects have run on Heroku and on AWS EC2.

Tools of the trade: Python, JavaScript, React, iOS, SQL, REST APIs, Snowflake, Power BI, large language models.

Part VII A builder inside finance and data

For ten years I've worked where product, data and technology meet, turning messy problems into products people use. Figure 2 sets the decade out to scale.

At Jefferies, as Product Strategist in Investment Banking, now Vice President, I led data scientists, engineers, delivery and business leaders to launch the firm's first LLM-powered application.4Before that, as a Data Analyst at Jefferies, I ran SQL and Python analysis for M&A sell-side deals and automated the team's performance reporting. I set strategy for a portfolio of fintech and banking software used across the investment bank, and use SQL-driven analysis to decide what to build next.

At Avero, as Product Owner and Data Analyst, I owned the sales, onboarding and billing platform end to end. I published nationwide reporting on the restaurant industry's recovery after COVID and moved the company's data into Snowflake and Power BI.

What carries through

Learning

From the DR to self-taught code to LLMs, I'm happiest as a beginner at something new.

Pushing my limits

When my cofounder left, I learned to build it myself. I keep choosing problems a little bigger than I am.

Caring about the work

My name is on what I ship. I sweat the details because the people using it deserve that.

Part VIII Building what's next with AI

Outside my day job, I'm building again, this time an AI startup. It's the same pull I felt at Binghamton, now with ten years of product and engineering experience behind it. I also invest in early-stage founders, including BoldVoice.5Away from the keyboard: skiing, judo, basketball, scuba diving and Formula 1.
