01

Two homes

Born in the US.
Raised for eleven years in the Dominican Republic.

My story starts with two homes. I was born in the United States, but I spent eleven years growing up in the Dominican Republic. It's where I learned that resourcefulness isn't a skill you study — it's one you live. When you don't have everything handed to you, you get creative with what you do have.

Growing up between two cultures and two languages taught me to adapt fast, listen closely, and feel at home in places that are new to me. That instinct has followed me into every room I've walked into since.

02

New York

Then came New York — and it doesn't wait for anyone.

Moving to New York City was a different kind of education. The pace, the ambition, the sheer number of people chasing something — it rewires you. NYC taught me that if you want something, you go get it, and you don't wait for permission or the perfect plan.

03

The non‑traditional path

I didn't take the straight road. I took the one that taught me the most.

My path to a four-year degree started at community college. A lot of people see that as a detour. For me, it was a launchpad. I put my head down, worked hard, and excelled — and earned my way to Binghamton University.

“Where you start doesn't decide where you finish. How hard you're willing to work does.”
04

Binghamton University

Where I fell for startups.

At Binghamton, I studied Financial Economics — but what really grabbed me was entrepreneurship. The idea that a small group of people with an idea and enough grit could build something from nothing and put it into people's hands was electric. I read everything I could, went to every talk I could find, and started looking at problems as things to be built, not just studied.

B.S. Financial Economics · Binghamton University (SUNY) · 2015

05

The magic book

My tech cofounder quit. So I learned to code.

My first real startup was an augmented reality magic book — a physical book that came alive when you pointed a phone at it. Characters leaping off the page, stories you could explore instead of just read.

Then, in the middle of building it, my technical cofounder quit.

That could've been the end of the story. Instead, it became the turning point. I didn't have someone to build it for me, so I decided I'd become someone who could build it myself. I taught myself to code — one tutorial, one bug, one late night at a time.

That moment taught me the most important lesson of my career: when you're blocked, learn your way through it.
